Public Opinion Towards the Impact of School Education in Chennai

Introduction

Indian education is a diverse landscape that combines traditional wisdom with modern learning. It spans a wide spectrum, from ancient gurukuls to cutting-edge technology-driven institutions. Challenges like access and quality persist, but India's commitment to education remains strong, fostering a new generation poised to lead in a globalized world.

The aim of this research is to mitigate an improved learning experience. This research investigates public opinion regarding the effectiveness and impact of school education in Chennai, India. Data was collected through a comprehensive survey administered to a diverse sample of Chennai residents, comprising parents, students, educators, and community members.

Descriptive research was conducted. 215 samples were collected through a convenient sampling method. The sample frame includes individuals from city, town, and village. Age, gender, educational qualification, employment status, and marital status are the independent variables taken for analyses in SPSS.

The factors as to which act as a benefit of schooling and factors which act as a negative aspect of schooling are the dependent variables of the analyses. Complex charts and correlation analyses are used as statistical tools for the analyses. The study reveals a range of opinions, with a significant portion of respondents expressing concerns about the need to improve teacher–student ratio, more extracurricular activities, implement professionalism, and more opportunities based on student's skills should be in Chennai schools. The study concludes that level of educational qualification and marital status impacts perceptions of school education in Chennai.

Objectives
  1. To study the opinion of the impact of today's school education with relation to different age groups.
  2. To examine the opinion of people with different educational qualification regarding the impact of today's school education.
Methodology
  • Research method: Empirical method
  • Sampling method: Convenient sampling. Sample size: 223
  • Sample frame: Chennai
  • Statistical tool: Bar graph
  • Dependent variables: Age, educational qualifications, place of residence, gender, occupation
  • Independent variables: Awareness on government initiatives, agreeability on the GST exemption

Discussion
  • People in the 20–30 age group believe that teacher harassment is a consequence of modern school education, while those under 20 years old think that effective communication is also a result of contemporary school education (Fig. 1).
  • Individuals under the age of 20 are in favor of enhancing the teacher–student ratio, while those aged 20 to 30 are highly supportive of such improvement (Fig. 2).
  • Individuals under 20 years of age strongly concur that extracurricular activities in school influence students, while those between 20 and 30 years old hold a general agreement on the impact of extracurricular activities on students (Fig. 3).
Conclusion

It is deduced from the study that individuals' age groups influence their opinions on the quality and effectiveness of school education. There is a gender-based difference in public opinion regarding school education. The level of educational qualification influences respondents' views on the education system, and marital status also impacts perceptions of school education in Chennai.

Professionals or better-educated respondents appear more aware of the pros and cons of present-day schooling and its impact on a child's life than those with less knowledge and awareness. There also exists a positive correlation between the factors that act as benefits of schooling and negative aspects of the same.

It is worth noting that the Right to Education (RTE) Act plays a pivotal role in India's educational landscape, providing a legal framework for the realization of every child's right to quality education — covering aspects from teacher deployment to curriculum development, with the aim of fostering equitable and inclusive education. The RTE Act stands as a testament to India's commitment to accessible, high-quality education for its youth, reflecting a vision of a brighter future for all. To bridge the existing gap, measures should be taken to educate and raise awareness about proper schooling, and policies should be framed to encourage a holistic approach in school.
