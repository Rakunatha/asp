Abstract

Digital transformation of justice delivery in India

This empirical study examines public awareness and utilization of e-Judiciary services at the Madras High Court — online case tracking, e-filing, virtual hearings — while assessing the growing role of Artificial Intelligence in judicial processes.

Data collected from 200 respondents in the Kilpauk region using structured questionnaires and SPSS analysis revealed uneven awareness, persistent digital barriers, and strong perceived benefits of digital judicial services.

Research Objectives

Key Barriers Identified

01

Digital Illiteracy

Many users lack basic skills to navigate online platforms, smartphones, or court portals effectively.

02

Language Barriers

Predominantly English interfaces limit accessibility for regional language speakers.

03

Internet Access

Uneven connectivity, especially among economically weaker sections and rural populations.

04

Fear of Errors

Psychological barriers — particularly among older users — of making irreversible procedural mistakes online.

05

Lack of Guidance

Insufficient on-site assistance within court premises, reducing user confidence in independent access.

06

Low AI Awareness

Limited public knowledge of AI-enabled tools like legal research assistance, translation, and case analytics.

Recommendations

🏛️

Infrastructure

Establish permanent help desks within court premises with trained staff to guide users through e-Judiciary services in regional languages.

📱

Technology

Develop user-friendly mobile applications with multilingual support and simplified navigation to reduce barriers for less digitally literate users.

🎓

Digital Literacy

Implement targeted training programmes for older citizens, women, and economically weaker groups, leveraging community networks and traditional channels.

🤖

Responsible AI Integration

Integrate AI tools for case management, legal translation, and decision-support with clear ethical frameworks, transparency, and accountability safeguards.

11

Conclusion

"Digital justice fulfils its potential only when technological advancement is matched by social inclusion — otherwise, e-Judiciary risks deepening existing inequalities rather than alleviating them."

While e-Judiciary services enjoy broad legitimacy and perceived usefulness, awareness and utilization remain uneven across age, gender, education, and occupation. AI integration holds transformative promise — but must be accompanied by human-centric outreach and robust policy support.
