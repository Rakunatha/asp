§ 01 — The Problem

Justice, delayed by design.

A single household dispute can touch marriage, maintenance, domestic-violence and guardianship law at once — often in different forums, each with its own pace.

Family Court Magistrate — Maintenance District — Guardianship Mediation Centre High Court — Appeals

Family law in India doesn't lack rules. It lacks a system to hold them together.

  • Cases outlive the events that caused them. A maintenance or custody dispute can stretch across years — longer than the marriage itself lasted.
  • The file lives everywhere and nowhere. Petitions, affidavits and prior orders are scattered across registries, desks and memory.
  • No one holds the whole picture. Not the litigant waiting for news, and often not the advocate juggling dozens of files.
§ 02 — The Gap

Digitised paperwork isn't a system.

Most legal-tech in this space stores documents and manages calendars. Very little models the workflow itself — the sequence of filings, forums and deadlines a family case has to move through.

This study asks a harder question: what would it take to make that path mappable, automated and intelligent — not just recorded?

§ 03 — System Architecture

Four layers, one case.

Every case enters once and is understood by all four layers — instead of being re-entered and re-tracked at each stage.

Intake & Classification

Identifies case type, applicable statute(s) and correct forum the moment a matter opens.

Workflow Engine

Models the case as a living process — stage, next action, owner — not a static file.

Intelligence Layer

Assists drafting, predicts deadlines, retrieves precedent tuned to family law.

Interface Layer

Gives advocate, litigant and registry a dedicated view of the same case.
