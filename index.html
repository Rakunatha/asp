Tracking Creation in the Age of AI

A Provenance-Based Framework for Authorship Attribution and Copyright Protection

Slide 2

The Law Is Asking for a Human

United States

Thaler v. Perlmutter (D.C. Cir., 2025): only humans can be authors. AI-assisted work is protected only for its human-authored contribution.

India

Section 2(d)(vi) names the person who "causes" a computer-generated work as author. Courts still look for human skill and judgment.

EU & UK

EU AI Act Art. 50 requires machine-readable marking of synthetic content. UK CDPA s.9(3) gives computer-generated works to the person making the arrangements.

Slide 3

Why Provenance Matters

Provenance proves process. Copyright asks about creativity.

  • Logs show who prompted what, with which model, and when.
  • Courts ask whether a human made creative choices: selecting, arranging, editing.
  • So provenance is evidence of human contribution, not a substitute for it.
  • Its real value: settling ownership disputes, supporting licensing, and enabling disclosure.

Slide 4

Review of Literature

  • Ownership models range from hybrid developer-user rights (Wangmo) to prompt-based ownership (Shafiq).
  • Technical approaches embed traceable metadata during generation (Khan et al.), reducing disputes in co-created work (Lauk-Dubitskiy).
  • Scholars call for a codified "machine-assisted author" category (Oliveira) with contribution-based thresholds (Ramos-Zaga).

Slide 5

The Study & Its Limits

Design

Empirical, descriptive survey. Five-point Likert questionnaire, 210 respondents, convenience sample.

Analysis

SPSS v21: chi-square tests and one-way ANOVA across age, gender, education, area and occupation.

Limits

Convenience sampling, self-report bias, cross-sectional design. Attitudes are measured, not causal effects.

Slide 7

The Way Forward

  • Standardise: interoperable provenance metadata across AI platforms.
  • Protect creators: privacy by design, so creators control their own logs (GDPR, India's DPDP Act 2023).
  • Avoid platform capture: fund open-source provenance infrastructure so evidence isn't owned by a few companies.
  • Pair with law reform: clear thresholds for human contribution, backed by provenance evidence.
