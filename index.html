Supreme Court of India  ·  (2004) 1 SCC 581

K. Balakrishnan

versus

K. Kamalam & Ors.

A landmark ruling on gift deeds, minor donees & irrevocability

The Story

A mother.
A gift.
A conflict.

Everything began in 1945 — and ended decades later in the Supreme Court.

The Facts

1945
Mother executes a registered gift deed transferring her share in property to her minor son (Balakrishnan) and minor daughter (Kamalam). She reserves the right to manage and enjoy the property during her lifetime.
Later
Mother executes a cancellation deed and a will in favour of the daughter only — attempting to revoke the original gift entirely.
After
Mother dies. The son sues, asserting title under the original 1945 gift deed.
2004
The Supreme Court of India settles the matter once and for all.

Three Questions Before the Court

The legal battle
came down to three
fundamental questions.

Question 01

Can a minor validly accept a gift?

Question 02

Is delivery of possession necessary for a valid gift of immovable property?

Question 03

Can a donor unilaterally revoke a completed gift?

Question 01  ·  Minor Donee

Can a minor
accept a gift?

The Transfer of Property Act, 1882 is silent on this. The courts had to reason from first principles.

The Court's Answer

Yes.

A minor is not legally incapable of receiving property. When a parent makes a beneficial gift to a child, acceptance can be presumed — the donor's role as natural guardian is enough. Acceptance may be express or implied.

Question 02  ·  Delivery of Possession

Must you hand
over possession
to gift property?

The mother kept the right to manage and enjoy the property for life. Did that invalidate the gift?

The Court's Answer

No.

A valid gift of immovable property does not require immediate transfer of possession. Ownership passes even if the donor reserves possession, management, or enjoyment for life. A life interest is perfectly compatible with a completed gift.

Question 03  ·  Revocation

Can a donor take
back a completed
gift?

The mother's cancellation deed and will attempted exactly this. Was it legally effective?

The Court's Answer

No.

Once validly made, accepted, and completed, a gift cannot be revoked unilaterally. The conditions for revocation under law were not satisfied. The cancellation deed was legally ineffective.

Ratio Decidendi

The Legal Principle

A gift in favour of a minor is valid if it is beneficial to the minor and is accepted expressly or impliedly — acceptance may be presumed when the donor is the minor's parent or guardian.

A donor may reserve a life interest without invalidating the gift.

A completed gift cannot be revoked unilaterally.
