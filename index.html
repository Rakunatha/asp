Case Brief No. 01 — Abstract & Aim

Can systems intelligence make family law practice faster, fairer, and more accessible?

The study designs and evaluates a systems-based legal workflow intelligence platform for Indian family law, applying artificial intelligence to analyse large datasets and inform legal decision-making across a sample of 220 legal professionals and stakeholders.

Aim

Design and develop a systems-based legal workflow intelligence platform capable of effectively supporting Indian family law practice through AI-driven analysis and insight generation.

SLIDE 03 / 09 — OBJECTIVES
Case Brief No. 03 — Review of Literature

Twenty studies, four recurring themes

Digital Judiciary Awareness

Public awareness of e-filing, virtual hearings and the National Judicial Data Grid remains fragmented and low despite high institutional usage, with usability and guidance cited as recurring gaps.

Sundaram & Iyer 2024 · Mehta & Rao 2021 · Krishnan 2010 · Priya 2013
AI Adoption & Ethics

Legal professionals show cautious acceptance of AI tools such as translation and case-summarisation software, driven by concerns over accountability and the need for clear regulatory frameworks.

Anand 2022 · Bhattacharya & Ghosh 2020 · Simshaw 2021
Access & Equity

Rural and economically weaker populations face compounding barriers — device access, connectivity and digital literacy — that limit uptake of e-Judiciary services regardless of awareness.

Joseph & Thomas 2022 · Sharma 2018 · Prabhavathi & Durai 2024
Global & Comparative Perspectives

Cross-jurisdictional research shows public trust in algorithmic adjudication still lags trust in human judges, even as chatbots and virtual assistants improve first-touch access to legal guidance.

Yalcin et al. 2022 · Fine et al. 2024 · OECD 2025 · UNESCO 2025

Age Trend

The 31–40 age bracket consistently shows the strongest awareness and engagement across every measure recorded.

Moderating Factor

Educational qualification repeatedly emerges as a moderating influence on awareness, even where the formal chi-square test is non-significant.

Gender Gap

Awareness is broadly shared across genders, but actual usage of digital legal services skews markedly male.

Occupation

Private-sector and self-employed respondents show the highest platform engagement; retired respondents show the lowest.

SLIDE 06 / 09 — FINDINGS
Case Brief No. 06 — Discussion, Limitations & Recommendations

Broad legitimacy, uneven reach

Digital Divide

  • Awareness is not merely infrastructural — confidence and fear of error drive exclusion among older users.
  • Lawyers and family remain the dominant awareness channel over institutional digital outreach.
  • Women show comparable awareness to men but markedly lower actual usage.

Limitations

  • Convenience sample of 220 limits generalisability across all relevant population groups.
  • Geographic concentration under-represents rural and remote populations.
  • Self-report and social desirability bias may shape responses on sensitive items.

Recommendations

  • Strengthen legislative and regulatory frameworks, enforced consistently across states.
  • Accelerate AI-driven monitoring tools alongside human help-desks and training.
  • Expand awareness campaigns into rural and underserved communities.
SLIDE 07 / 09 — DISCUSSION
Case Brief No. 07 — Conclusion

Education and technology, together, close the access-to-justice gap

Across 220 respondents, the 31–40 age group and graduate-level participants showed the strongest engagement with systems-based legal workflow intelligence, while rural and lower-education respondents lagged behind. The findings confirm the study's objectives and point to three pillars for an integrated response.

01· Legislative reform
02· Digital infrastructure investment
03· Sustained community outreach
