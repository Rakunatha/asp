Abstract & Objectives

Research At a Glance

This study investigates AI's transformative potential in IPR contract drafting — examining how smart contracts and automated licensing can reshape the tech-legal landscape. Survey of 225 respondents across diverse demographics, analysed via SPSS v21.

Objectives
  • Investigate AI's role in IPR drafting and its potential to cut time and costs.
  • Examine smart contracts' impact on contract accuracy and negotiation speed.
  • Analyse benefits and risks — efficiency gains vs. regulatory challenges.
  • Develop recommendations and regulatory frameworks for AI-driven drafting.

  • Awareness is strongest among the 31–40 age group and higher-educated respondents.
  • Rural and lower-education demographics show comparatively lower awareness — a clear digital divide.
  • Convergence across groups suggests AI-IPR awareness transcends socio-demographic barriers.
  • Targeted digital outreach strategies are essential for under-informed segments.
  • Education and technology-based interventions remain the critical enablers for adoption.

Conclusion & References

Closing Remarks

🎓

Strongest Engagement

Graduate-level & 31–40 age group respondents showed the deepest awareness.

⚠️

Limitations

Convenience sample; geographic concentration; cross-sectional design only.

🏛️

Three Policy Pillars

Legislative reform  ·  Digital infrastructure investment  ·  Sustained community outreach

Conclusion: AI has demonstrable potential to transform IPR contract drafting. Education and technology-based interventions are the critical enablers. Longitudinal, multi-state research is needed to build a comprehensive evidence base.
