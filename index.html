02 · Abstract

Abstract & Overview

Judicial review of IPR decisions is crucial in India's legal system. Constitutional courts under Articles 32 & 226 ensure administrative tribunals act fairly and within procedural propriety.

The abolition of IPAB in 2021 transferred functions to High Courts, intensifying debates on the scope of judicial review.

Judicial ReviewIPR Admin TribunalsIndia IPABHigh Courts

Intellectual Property Rights have emerged as a critical component of modern economic and technological development. Administrative bodies like the erstwhile IPAB adjudicated IPR disputes — but following the abolition of IPAB in 2021, functions transferred to High Courts.

Judicial review acts as a safeguard against arbitrariness, jurisdictional errors, and natural justice violations, maintaining the rule of law in administrative decision-making.

03 · Objectives
03

Research Objectives

01

Examine Current Process

Analyse the existing judicial review process of administrative tribunals in India, specifically in the context of intellectual property rights decisions and their constitutional backing.

02

Role of Judicial Review

Evaluate how judicial review ensures that administrative tribunals make decisions aligned with the law without infringing on individual rights or exceeding jurisdiction.

03

Strengths & Weaknesses

Identify the gaps and strengths in the current review process and recommend targeted areas for legislative reform and institutional improvement.

04

Systemic Impact

Investigate the broader impact of judicial review on tribunal functioning and the long-term implications for accountability and fairness in the Indian legal system.

07 · Conclusion
⚖️

Conclusion & Policy Implications

This study critically examines how judicial review of IPR decisions rendered by administrative tribunals in India operates within a constitutional framework. The abolition of IPAB in 2021 has intensified the discourse on the role and scope of judicial review, with High Courts now balancing deference to technical expertise against constitutional principles.

Findings suggest that legislative reforms are urgently needed to strengthen the judicial review process, ensure tribunal accountability, and achieve a more just and fair society for IP rights holders and innovators.
